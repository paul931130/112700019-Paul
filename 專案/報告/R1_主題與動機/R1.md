# R1: Topic and Motivation

Corresponding manuscript section: Introduction / Motivation (`../../manuscript/main.tex` §1)

## 1. Background Problem

Traditional stock price/return prediction models usually treat each stock as its own
independent time series — looking only at its own past prices, volume, and fundamentals. This
is convenient, but it discards a signal that is easy to observe and often economically
meaningful: stocks in the same industry, the same supply chain, or held by the same funds
often move together, because a shock to one company is frequently a shock to the whole group.
A model that treats stocks as independent is, by design, blind to this channel.

## 2. Paper and Method

Paper being replicated: Feng, Fuli, Xiangnan He, Xiang Wang, Cheng Luo, Yiqun Liu, and
Tat-Seng Chua. "Temporal Relational Ranking for Stock Prediction." *ACM Transactions on
Information Systems* 37, no. 2 (2019): Article 27. https://doi.org/10.1145/3309547.

In one sentence: Feng et al. propose Relational Stock Ranking (RSR), encoding relations
between stocks (industry relation, Wikidata relation) into a ranking task via Temporal Graph
Convolution, replacing the prior approach of treating stocks as independent entities. The goal
is to predict the relative ranking of next-day stock returns (not the absolute return value),
since for a long-short or top-k trading strategy the relative ranking matters more than the
predicted value itself.

## 3. Why This Paper

- **Journal tier**: NYCU CS A-tier journal list, entry 11 (`journal-ranking/交大資工A級期刊_20200910Updated.xlsx`).
- **Reproducibility**: the official code runs (cloned locally, TF1→TF2 compatibility patched,
  both baseline and RSR have been trained to completion with results recorded — see
  `../R3_模型與實驗設計/PROGRESS.md`).
- **Personal connection**: I'm separately building a **multi-agent system for trading/investment
  decisions** — multiple agents each handle a different task (e.g. technical analysis,
  news/sentiment analysis, risk management), whose outputs are then aggregated into a trading
  decision. This system currently **lacks any "cross-stock relation" type of signal**: each
  agent essentially analyzes a single ticker in isolation, with no agent modeling which stocks
  it moves together with as part of its input. The RSR paper demonstrates a concrete,
  implementable way to do this — using an industry/Wikidata relation graph to turn "relatedness"
  into an actual model feature (a relation graph plus a learned attention weight over other
  stocks' representations), rather than an abstract claim that "stocks move together."
  Replicating this paper is a first check of whether this kind of "relation signal" is actually
  useful on the most basic ranking task, before there's any basis for deciding whether to wire
  a similar relation feature into a "cross-stock relation" agent in the multi-agent system.

  **Important scope note**: this project's replication work **does not include** wiring RSR into
  the multi-agent system, nor any architectural modification or extension of RSR — it is a
  straight replication of Feng et al. (2019)'s two models, baseline (Rank_LSTM) and RSR
  (relation_rank_lstm), comparing the effect of the relation graph being present or absent. The
  multi-agent system is only the personal motivation for "why this paper," not part of this
  project's research scope.

## 4. Research Question (RQ)

- **RQ1 (replication)**: Does relational information (industry relation) actually improve stock
  ranking prediction performance, relative to a baseline that does not use relations?

## 5. Expected Contribution

Replicate an already-published, high-quality result, and honestly check whether the original
paper's conclusion — that relational information improves ranking performance — holds up under
a fresh run. This includes the process of working through environment issues (TF1→TF2
compatibility) and data-quality issues (a missing-value sentinel, ETFs mixed into the relation
graph), which are themselves worth documenting as part of a replication study (see
`../R2_資料與EDA/`, `../R3_模型與實驗設計/PROGRESS.md`).

# Candidate Papers Relevant to Dynamic Cross-Stock Relations

Source list: [citable-items-181-raw.md](citable-items-181-raw.md). The list was downloaded from a journal table of contents, but the source does not identify the journal or issue. Add the source URL here before using Chicago-style citations.

Most of the 181 papers concern corporate finance or governance and are not directly relevant to this project (relations between stocks and return/ranking prediction). The most relevant candidates are:

## Most Relevant: How Relations and Networks Transmit Information to Stock Prices

| # | Title | Relevance to this project |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | Overlapping Ownership Along the Supply Chain | Shared ownership links along the supply chain are a type of non-price relation, similar to RSR's Wikidata/industry graph. This could provide empirical support for the R1 motivation on why relations matter. |
| 60 | The Downstream Impact of Upstream Tariffs: Evidence from Investment Decisions in Supply Chains | Examines transmission of shocks between upstream and downstream firms. It supports the idea that relation strength can change with events and directly speaks to the dynamic-relation hypothesis. |
| 98 | Is There Smart Money? How Information in the Commodity Futures Market Is Priced into the Cross Section of Stock Returns with Delay | Studies delayed information transmission across markets into stock returns. Its lead–lag perspective is close to RSR's ranking task and could be used for methodological comparison. |
| 95 | Information Spillover and Corporate Policies: The Case of Listed Options | Provides an empirical design for studying information spillovers and may offer guidance on defining relation strength. |

## Also Relevant: Machine Learning and Cross-Sectional Return Prediction

| # | Title | Relevance to this project |
|---|---|---|
| 181 | Uncovering Sparsity and Heterogeneity in Firm-Level Return Predictability Using Machine Learning | Directly addresses firm-level return predictability with machine learning. It could be included in R1 and compared with RSR's ranking-based prediction. |
| 85 | Estimating Stock Market Betas via Machine Learning | Uses machine learning to estimate common risk exposures (betas) across stocks, conceptually related to measuring relation strength. |
| 12 | Deep Learning in Characteristics-Sorted Factor Models | Applies deep learning to asset-pricing factor models and offers a methodological comparison with RSR's Temporal GCN. |

## Not Recommended

The remaining papers (on CEO compensation, corporate governance, bank regulation, IPOs, and other topics) are farther from the focus on dynamic relations between stocks. Unless the literature review needs broader motivation for why firms have real-world connections (for example, governance links), there is no need to force them into the review.
